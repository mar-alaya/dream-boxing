New to Boxing? Start Here!

Boxing can seem complicated when you're just getting started. There are different stances, punches, rules, weight classes and training methods to learn. Don't worry — everyone starts somewhere.

The Four Basic Punches

Jab

A fast and straight punch thrown with the lead hand.

Cross

A powerful straight punch thrown with the rear hand.

Crochet / Hook

A curved punch designed to attack from the side.

Uppercut

A punch that travels upward towards the opponent.

Boxing Legends

Throughout boxing history, certain fighters have changed the sport forever. Their achievements, personalities and fighting styles continue to inspire new generations.

Muhammad Ali

Known for his incredible speed, charisma and confidence, Muhammad Ali became one of the most recognizable athletes in the history of sport.

Mike Tyson

Famous for his explosive punching power and aggressive style, Mike Tyson became the youngest heavyweight champion in boxing history.

Claressa Shields

One of the most accomplished female boxers in history. A two-time Olympic gold medalist, she has won world championships across multiple weight divisions and is known for her technical ability, speed and competitive mentality.

Sugar Ray Leonard

One of the greatest boxers of his generation, Leonard combined speed, technique and intelligence to defeat some of the best fighters of his era.

How does boxing work?

What is the objective of boxing?

The objective is to land legal punches on the opponent while avoiding their attacks. A boxer can win by knockout, technical knockout or by receiving a higher score from the judges.

How many rounds are there in a boxing match?

Professional boxing matches can last up to 12 rounds, with each round lasting three minutes. Amateur and some other competitions may use different formats.

What punches are allowed in boxing?

Boxers can use legal punches such as the jab, cross, hook and uppercut. Punches must land on the front or sides of the opponent's head or body above the belt.

Punches to the back of the head, neck or below the belt are illegal. Hitting an opponent who is down or using the elbows, head or other illegal techniques is also prohibited.

What happens after a knockdown?

The referee starts a count while the knocked-down boxer tries to get back on their feet. If the boxer cannot continue before the count ends, the opponent wins by knockout.

How do judges score a boxing match?

Judges score each round based on factors such as effective punching, defense, ring control and effective aggression. The boxer who wins the most rounds usually receives the higher score.
